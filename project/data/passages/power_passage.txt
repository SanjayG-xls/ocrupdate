Machines and people both do work when they move objects. Work is done when a force moves an object through a distance, and it is measured in joules. The work done in a unit of time is called power. Power is calculated by dividing the work done by the time taken to do it. The basic unit of power is the watt (W), and large machines are usually rated in kilowatts (kW). For example, a motor that does 600 joules of work in 5 seconds has a power of 120 W. If the time stays the same, a machine with a greater work must have a higher power. If two machines do the same work, the one with the smaller time has a higher power. That is why a lift that is faster needs a more powerful motor. An electricity meter in a home records the energy used by all the appliances. Knowing power helps us compare machines and save electricity.
