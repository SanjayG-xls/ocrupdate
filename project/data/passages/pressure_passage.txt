When we push on a surface, the effect of the push depends on how much area it is spread over. A push or a pull on an object is called a force. The force acting on a unit area of a surface is called pressure. Pressure is calculated by dividing the force acting on a surface by the area of that surface. The basic unit of pressure is newton per square metre (N/m²), which is also called the pascal (Pa). For example, a box that pushes on the floor with a force of 600 newtons over an area of 2 square metres exerts a pressure of 300 Pa. If the area stays the same, a box with a greater force must have a higher pressure. If two objects push with the same force, the one with the smaller area exerts a higher pressure. That is why a sharp knife cuts more easily than a blunt one. A spring balance measures the force acting on an object. Knowing pressure helps us design tools, shoes and building foundations.
