Every object is made of matter, and different materials pack different amounts of matter into the same space. The mass of an object is the amount of matter it contains. The volume of an object is the amount of space it occupies. The mass of a unit volume of a material is called its density. Density is calculated by dividing the mass of an object by its volume. The basic unit of density is kilogram per cubic metre (kg/m³), but in the laboratory density is usually given in grams per cubic centimetre (g/cm³). For example, a block that has a mass of 120 grams and a volume of 40 cubic centimetres has a density of 3 g/cm³. If the volume stays the same, an object with a greater mass must have a higher density. If two objects have the same mass, the one with the smaller volume is denser. A balance in the laboratory measures the mass of an object, while a measuring cylinder measures the volume of a liquid. Knowing density helps us predict whether an object will float or sink in water.
