--- L3 explanation (original) ---
    An object is said to be in motion when its position changes with time. The distance an object
    covers in a unit of time is called its speed. Speed is calculated by dividing the total distance
    covered by the total time taken. The basic unit of speed is metre per second (m/s), but speed on
    roads is usually given in kilometres per hour (km/h). For example, a bus that covers 120
    kilometres in 2 hours has an average speed of 60 km/h. If the time taken stays the same, a
    vehicle that covers a greater distance must have a higher speed. If two vehicles cover the same
    distance, the one that takes less time is faster. Most vehicles do not move at a constant speed;
    they speed up and slow down along the way. That is why we usually talk about average speed. A
    speedometer in a vehicle shows its speed at that moment, while an odometer measures the total
    distance it has travelled. Knowing speed helps us plan journeys and estimate how long they will
    take.
